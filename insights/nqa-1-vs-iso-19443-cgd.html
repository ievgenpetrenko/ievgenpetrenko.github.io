NQA-1 vs. ISO 19443: Why ISO Certification Does Not Replace Commercial Grade Dedication

Why management-system certification and item acceptance are different assurance questions in nuclear procurement.

By Ievgen Petrenko | Published 4 October 2026, 19:18 CEST

The nuclear supply chain is expanding.

Large Generation III+ projects, SMRs and advanced reactor programmes increasingly depend on companies whose experience was built in automotive, aerospace, electronics, advanced manufacturing and other high-performance industries.

Many of these organisations already operate mature quality management systems.

Some are certified to ISO 9001.

An increasing number are also implementing or certifying against ISO 19443.

That leads to an understandable question:

If a supplier is certified to ISO 9001 or ISO 19443, is Commercial Grade Dedication still necessary?

The answer depends on the item, its intended safety function, the regulatory framework and the procurement route.

But one principle is clear:

ISO certification does not, by itself, replace Commercial Grade Dedication where dedication is required for acceptance of a commercial-grade item as a basic component.

That distinction matters increasingly as international nuclear programmes expand their supplier base.

Three frameworks, different purposes

ISO 9001, ISO 19443 and ASME NQA-1 all address quality.

But they do not perform the same function.

ISO 9001 provides a general quality management system framework applicable across industries.

ISO 19443 builds on ISO 9001 for organisations in the nuclear energy supply chain supplying products and services important to nuclear safety, ITNS.

ASME NQA-1 provides detailed quality assurance requirements for nuclear facility applications, including design, procurement, inspection, records, control of nonconforming items and commercial-grade dedication.

Similar quality objectives do not make the frameworks regulatory equivalents.

ISO itself states that ISO 19443 requirements are complementary to, not alternatives to, applicable customer, statutory and regulatory requirements.

That principle is fundamental for nuclear procurement.

What ISO 19443 adds

ISO 19443 is much more than conventional ISO 9001 applied inside a nuclear company.

It introduces nuclear-specific requirements and concepts into the management system of organisations supplying products and services important to nuclear safety.

Its value is significant.

It can strengthen:

  • nuclear safety culture,
  • application of the graded approach,
  • management of ITNS products and services,
  • competence,
  • traceability,
  • supply-chain controls,
  • prevention of counterfeit, fraudulent and suspect items,
  • management of nuclear-specific risks.

For companies entering the nuclear sector, that provides a strong foundation.

But there is an important boundary.

ISO 19443 certification demonstrates conformity of an organisation's management system.

It does not automatically establish that every component supplied by that organisation is acceptable as a safety-related basic component under another regulatory and QA framework.

That decision depends on the applicable project requirements.

Start with the safety function

The procurement question should therefore not begin with:

Which certificate does the supplier have?

It should begin with:

What does the item need to do?

Then:

  • What is its safety function?
  • How is it classified?
  • What regulatory framework applies?
  • What quality assurance requirements apply?
  • How will compliance be demonstrated?

Only after those questions are answered can the supplier's quality system and procurement route be evaluated properly.

The logical chain is:

safety function → classification → regulatory basis → QA requirements → procurement route → verification → objective evidence

That sequence matters more than the certificate on the supplier's wall.

Why Commercial Grade Dedication exists

Commercial Grade Dedication addresses a specific procurement problem.

Commercial manufacturers can produce technically excellent components without operating under a nuclear quality assurance programme applicable to basic components.

Those items may include:

  • bearings,
  • motors,
  • electrical relays,
  • transmitters,
  • cables,
  • valves,
  • instrumentation,
  • fasteners,
  • electronic components,
  • industrial control equipment.

The NRC defines commercial-grade dedication as the process by which a commercial- grade item is accepted for use as a basic component.

The purpose is to provide reasonable assurance that the item will perform its intended safety function.

That assurance is achieved by identifying the item's critical characteristics and verifying their acceptability through appropriate inspections, tests, analyses or other accepted dedication methods.

This distinction is important.

CGD is not simply an additional supplier audit.

It is an acceptance process connected to a defined item or service and its intended safety function.

ISO certification does not remove that need

Imagine that a manufacturer has an ISO 9001 certified management system.

Its processes may be mature.

Its products may have excellent industrial reliability.

That still does not demonstrate that a particular commercial-grade item satisfies the critical characteristics necessary for a specific nuclear safety function.

ISO 19443 moves the supplier considerably closer to the nuclear environment.

The organisation already works within a management system designed for the nuclear supply chain and ITNS products or services.

But the same principle remains.

Certification of the organisation and acceptance of a specific item are different assurance questions.

Where a commercial-grade item is being accepted as a basic component under the applicable NRC-style procurement framework, dedication remains the mechanism for providing the required reasonable assurance.

One qualification is essential

This is where the discussion needs precision.

CGD is not required merely because a supplier holds ISO 9001 or ISO 19443 certification.

Nor is every product from an ISO-certified company automatically a candidate for dedication.

If an item is supplied under an appropriately qualified nuclear QA programme that meets the applicable procurement requirements for that scope, the procurement path may be different.

The key issue is how the item is being procured and accepted.

ISO 9001 or ISO 19443 certification does not eliminate CGD when the procurement strategy relies on dedication of a commercial-grade item for use as a basic component.

That is very different from saying that every ISO-certified supplier requires CGD.

What CGD actually demonstrates

A dedication process typically begins with the item's intended safety function.

From there, the dedicating entity determines the characteristics that need to be verified to provide reasonable assurance that the item can perform that function.

Depending on the item and dedication strategy, verification may involve:

  • technical evaluation,
  • identification of critical characteristics,
  • inspections,
  • testing,
  • analyses,
  • source verification,
  • commercial-grade surveys,
  • documentation review,
  • acceptance records,
  • configuration control.

NRC guidance explicitly connects dedication with identification and verification of critical characteristics.

The central distinction can therefore be expressed simply:

Management-system certification provides confidence in how an organisation manages its processes.

Dedication provides assurance for the acceptance of a particular commercial-grade item or service for a defined nuclear application.

They are related.

They are not interchangeable.

CGD does not qualify the supplier

Another important distinction is between supplier qualification and item dedication.

CGD should not be described simply as a way of turning a commercial supplier into a nuclear-qualified supplier.

That is not what it does.

The dedication process provides an acceptance route for the relevant commercial- grade item or service.

The supplier may continue to operate under its commercial management system.

The dedicating entity remains responsible for carrying out the activities necessary to establish reasonable assurance that the item will perform its intended safety function.

This creates an important procurement option.

The nuclear project can use capable commercial manufacturers without requiring every manufacturer to implement a complete nuclear QA programme for every product.

Why ISO 19443 still matters

None of this reduces the value of ISO 19443.

Quite the opposite.

An ISO 19443 supplier starts from a very different level of nuclear maturity than a conventional commercial supplier.

In practice, a mature ISO 19443 system can reduce the organisational gap between a conventional commercial supplier and a supplier operating within a nuclear project environment, even though it does not replace project-specific acceptance requirements.

The organisation is already operating within a nuclear-sector management-system framework.

That can improve:

  • understanding of nuclear safety culture,
  • traceability,
  • documentation discipline,
  • supplier management,
  • competence management,
  • application of the graded approach,
  • control of nuclear-specific processes.

This can make interfaces with nuclear customers more effective.

It may also reduce some of the organisational and supply-chain risks associated with introducing commercial industry into nuclear delivery.

But ISO 19443 deliberately remains complementary to customer and regulatory requirements.

That is why certification should be treated as an important capability indicator, not as a universal procurement acceptance mechanism.

Responsibility remains with the nuclear purchaser

A certificate does not transfer procurement responsibility.

Nuclear QA places strong emphasis on procurement document control, control of purchased items and services, verification and acceptance.

ASME describes procurement and commercial-grade dedication among the core processes covered by NQA-1, while NRC oversight of Appendix B programmes includes procurement controls and commercial-grade dedication activities.

For the organisation responsible for the applicable nuclear QA programme, the fundamental question remains:

Have we established adequate assurance that the purchased item or service meets the applicable requirements?

Supplier certification can support that determination.

It cannot substitute for it.

This becomes important for localisation

The distinction has major implications for nuclear localisation.

Countries developing new nuclear programmes want domestic companies to participate.

Many of those companies already have excellent industrial capabilities.

Some operate ISO 9001 systems.

Others may adopt ISO 19443 as they move deeper into nuclear supply chains.

But requiring every capable industrial supplier to immediately implement the same nuclear QA programme can unnecessarily restrict participation.

The opposite approach is equally problematic.

Treating ordinary commercial certification as sufficient for every safety-significant application can create gaps in assurance.

The better approach is scope-specific.

Depending on the item and regulatory requirements, the procurement strategy may involve:

  • procurement from a qualified nuclear supplier,
  • supplier development,
  • ISO 19443 implementation,
  • project-specific qualification,
  • source surveillance,
  • inspection and testing,
  • Commercial Grade Dedication,
  • or a combination of these controls.

The objective is not maximum control.

It is the right level of assurance for the actual safety function and procurement route.

The future supply chain will be mixed

Future nuclear supply chains are unlikely to operate under one universal quality framework.

They will include organisations working under:

  • ISO 9001,
  • ISO 19443,
  • ASME NQA-1,
  • national nuclear QA requirements,
  • project-specific quality programmes,
  • and combinations of them.

ISO 19443:2018 remains the published international standard, including its 2024 climate-action amendment, while a second edition is under development.

The challenge for nuclear projects will therefore not be to force every supplier into one identical model.

It will be to understand the interfaces between different assurance frameworks and translate them into controlled procurement strategies.

Quality assurance should enable the supply chain

This is where nuclear quality becomes strategic.

Over-specification can increase cost, reduce competition and prevent capable industrial companies from entering the nuclear market.

Under-specification can create gaps in traceability, verification and regulatory evidence.

Neither extreme is desirable.

The purpose of nuclear QA is not to produce the maximum amount of documentation.

It is to establish proportionate, traceable and defensible assurance.

That requires understanding both the capability of the supplier and the significance of the item being supplied.

Bottom line

ISO 9001, ISO 19443, NQA-1 and Commercial Grade Dedication should not be viewed as competing alternatives.

They perform different functions within the assurance system.

ISO 9001 provides a general quality management foundation.

ISO 19443 strengthens that foundation for organisations supplying products and services important to nuclear safety.

NQA-1 provides detailed nuclear quality assurance requirements for nuclear facility applications, including procurement and commercial-grade dedication processes.

Commercial Grade Dedication provides an acceptance pathway for commercial-grade items or services that are to be used as basic components under the applicable regulatory framework.

The key question is therefore not:

NQA-1 or ISO 19443?

It is:

What is the safety function, what requirements apply, and what evidence is needed to demonstrate that the item can perform that function?

Once that is clear, supplier certification, qualification and dedication can each be used for what they were designed to do.

And that is where nuclear quality stops being a certificate discussion and becomes part of procurement strategy, supply-chain integration and nuclear safety assurance.

This article discusses general nuclear quality assurance and procurement principles using public standards and regulatory guidance. Applicability of NQA-1, ISO 19443, Commercial Grade Dedication or other controls depends on the applicable regulatory, contractual and project-specific requirements.
