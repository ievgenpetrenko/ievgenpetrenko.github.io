Executive perspective

THE REAL COST OF NUCLEAR QA IS NOT ONLY WHAT WE SPEND ON CONTROLS. IT IS THE COST OF IMPORTANT DECISIONS MADE BEFORE THE RIGHT EVIDENCE EXISTS.

A nuclear project may have rigorous QA arrangements and still encounter avoidable rework when a technical requirement, supplier capability or acceptance criterion is not established at the right decision point. This is a delivery-system challenge, not an indictment of any standard or organization.

Over three decades, ASME NQA-1 has developed its nuclear QA provisions while ISO 9001 has continued to evolve as a general industrial QMS standard. ISO 19443 adds nuclear-specific requirements for organizations supplying products and services important to nuclear safety (ITNS). Together, the standards offer complementary approaches, but they are not universally interchangeable.

The strategic question is not which certificate is most prestigious. It is how suppliers and nuclear projects select the appropriate requirements, demonstrate technical capability and preserve assurance continuity across interfaces.

A certified QMS is not the same as a qualified nuclear supply capability.
The optimization principle is not fewer requirements. It is the right evidence, the right independent verification where required, and the right decision at the right time.

Five conclusions for decision-makers

  1. Classify by function, not vocabulary: preserve the design and licensing classification across U.S., IAEA and ISO frameworks.
  2. Specify the governing obligations: code, QA edition, acceptance criteria and contract flow-down before releasing the affected scope.
  3. Qualify actual capability: a QMS certificate is not a substitute for relevant technical qualifications.
  4. Make procurement, execution and acceptance decisions evidence-ready without replacing mandatory hold points.
  5. Measure prevented late changes, rework, qualification gaps and evidence completeness, not paperwork volume.

Editorial scope: This is a strategic comparison and practical interpretation of published overviews and industry requirements, not a clause-by-clause redline of licensed standards or an official regulatory interpretation. Edition applicability depends on the governing licensing and contract basis.

I. Standards evolution: what actually changed?

ASME NQA-1, 1994 to 2026

The 1994 edition provides an important historical reference point. Successive editions have refined an established nuclear QA framework. The comparison below distinguishes long-standing QA principles from confirmed updates in the 2026 edition; it does not claim to identify the first appearance of each individual requirement in the intervening editions.

Historical checkpoint: An ASME technical presentation identifies the four-part reformatting in 1994, the major performance-based restructuring and NQA-1/NQA-2 integration in 1997, NRC-comment incorporation in 2008 and new technology/practices in 2012. These are historical milestones, not a substitute for comparing the actual clause text of each licensed edition. ASME historical presentation.

Design control, audits, procurement controls, QA records, special process control and corrective action are long-standing features of nuclear QA. The 2026 edition should not be portrayed as introducing the concepts of effectiveness or software QA for the first time. See the ASME 2026 summary.

Regulatory distinction: A newly issued ASME edition is not automatically endorsed by the NRC or incorporated into a particular U.S. licensing basis. The applicable version and any exceptions must be confirmed.

ISO 9001:2015 versus ISO 9001:2026

ISO published ISO 9001:2026 on 16 September 2026. It retains the process-based QMS framework while clarifying leadership and organizational expectations, notably quality culture, ethical behaviour, change management, and the treatment of risks and opportunities.

ISO 19443:2018 versus Amendment 1:2024

ISO 19443:2018 establishes a nuclear-specific QMS for ITNS suppliers based on ISO 9001:2015. Its 2024 amendment addresses climate-change relevance to organizational context. It is not a comprehensive revision of ITNS classification, CGD, nuclear safety culture or supplier qualification.

II. The 25 critical questions

Each answer combines a standards-aware interpretation with a practical strategic takeaway. These are management and engineering questions, not verbatim extracts from copyrighted standards.

Governance and applicability

01Does a newly published edition automatically become mandatory?

No. The governing standard and edition follow the applicable licensing basis, approved QA programme, procurement documents and contracts. Publication of NQA-1-2026 does not independently replace an edition already committed to a project. Similarly, the draft second edition of ISO 19443 is not a current compliance baseline.

Strategic point: Separate publication, regulatory acceptance and contractual applicability.

02How do ASME NQA-1 and ISO 19443 differ?

NQA-1 defines detailed nuclear quality assurance programme requirements for nuclear facility applications. ISO 19443 establishes a nuclear-specific, ISO 9001:2015-based QMS for organizations supplying products and services important to nuclear safety (ITNS). Their objectives overlap, but their detailed provisions and acceptance regimes are not automatically equivalent.

Strategic point: Compare obligations individually, not by certificate titles.

03Can one corporate QMS address multiple frameworks?

Yes. Common processes for document control, competence, supplier management and corrective action can be integrated if each applicable requirement remains covered and demonstrably implemented. Project-specific controls, independent verification, technical code provisions and approved regulatory commitments must remain identifiable.

Strategic point: Integrate common processes; preserve specific obligations.

04Does certification demonstrate nuclear project readiness?

Not on its own. A certificate provides evidence of conformity within a defined certification scope. Project readiness may also require design competence, manufacturing capacity, qualified special processes, product-specific verification, customer qualification and appropriate procurement acceptance arrangements.

Strategic point: A certified QMS is not the same as qualified nuclear supply capability.

05Should every supplier adopt the newest edition immediately?

Not necessarily. Assess the relevant changes against existing commitments and planned future work. New provisions may enhance performance, but adoption can require approval or contractual changes. Manage the current compliance baseline separately from voluntary improvement and future transition.

Strategic point: Use controlled delta assessments rather than uncontrolled re-baselining.

Safety and classification

06How do the frameworks address nuclear safety culture?

ISO 19443 explicitly includes nuclear safety culture requirements. NQA-1 sets organizational and technical controls that support rigorous nuclear QA. Neither a policy statement nor completion of training alone demonstrates a healthy safety culture; observable decisions, reporting, escalation and treatment of concerns matter.

Strategic point: Evaluate behavior and decisions, not only declarations.

07Does U.S. Safety-Related classification cover only physical equipment?

The formal 10 CFR 50.2 definition of Safety-Related applies to SSCs credited with specified design-basis functions. Associated design, analyses, fabrication, inspection, testing and other services may also be subject to QA or reporting controls. The NRC definition of basic component expressly includes relevant safety-related services. The SSC classification and the controls on associated activities should be distinguished.

Strategic point: Distinguish safety classification from the assurance obligations governing the work.

08Why do U.S. ITS/SR, ISO ITNS and IAEA terms mean different things?

They answer different questions: U.S. SR identifies defined reactor SSC functions; broader U.S. ITS is context-dependent; the IAEA describes categories of plant equipment; ISO 19443 uses ITNS within a nuclear supply-chain QMS. 10 CFR 50.2 has no single universal definition of ITS.

Strategic point: Map governing functions and obligations, not names. See the detailed crosswalk.

09Does IAEA “safety related item” mean U.S. “Safety-Related SSC”?

No. IAEA Figure 3 treats Safety Related Items as distinct from Safety Systems and Safety Features for Design Extension Conditions. U.S. SR SSCs may perform safety-system functions. English terms do not establish regulatory equivalence.

Strategic point: Preserve the approved design classification and applicable technical requirements.

10Who establishes classification and the resulting QA requirements?

The approved design and licensing classification process, together with assigned organizational authority, establishes the safety functions and classifications. Responsible project organizations translate these into technical codes, QA provisions, supplier qualification, inspection and test requirements, and contractual evidence. NQA-1 is not itself a safety classification code. A supplier must not independently downgrade an approved classification.

Strategic point: Approve the classification-to-procurement crosswalk before releasing affected work.

Engineering and procurement

11What makes design control effective?

Traceable design inputs, appropriately verified outputs, controlled changes and clear acceptance criteria. NQA-1 contains specific nuclear design QA provisions; ISO 19443 applies QMS design and development requirements with nuclear-specific additions. Drawing release should not be mistaken for demonstrated design maturity.

Strategic point: Verify maturity before procurement and work release.

12Why is configuration management essential?

Because a changed requirement, drawing, material or operating environment can invalidate earlier technical or acceptance decisions. Changes must be evaluated for their consequences across engineering, qualification, procurement, inspection, construction and retained evidence.

Strategic point: Track impacts across the whole requirement-to-acceptance chain.

13What is the key quality risk at engineering-to-procurement handover?

An applicable requirement may fail to reach the supplier in a usable, contractually binding form. Classification, technical conditions, inspection obligations, verification criteria and record requirements must be transferred correctly before work is authorized.

Strategic point: A supplier cannot implement requirements the customer has not defined and communicated.

14How is a nuclear supplier qualified?

By evidence of capability for the defined scope, including QMS performance, technical competence, personnel and process qualifications, relevant performance history, external-provider controls and purchaser acceptance. An ISO or ASME certificate may support evaluation without completing it.

Strategic point: Qualification is scope-specific, not generic.

15Are commercial-grade dedication approaches interchangeable?

No. NQA-1 contains specific CGD requirements in modern editions, while ISO 19443 addresses commercial-grade concepts within its own supply-chain framework. Similar terminology does not prove equivalence of engineering evaluation, critical characteristics or permitted acceptance methods.

Strategic point: Apply the actual safety function and governing dedication route.

Execution and evidence

16How should welding, NDE and other special processes be controlled?

Through qualified procedures, competent personnel, controlled parameters, equipment, inspection and records, as required. QA standards establish management and assurance controls; applicable construction codes and technical specifications establish additional execution and acceptance requirements.

Strategic point: A QMS does not replace a technical construction code.

17What does independent verification require?

Independence is determined by the applicable activity and specific requirements. NQA-1 addresses independence for relevant inspections and design verification. ISO 19443 requires suitable verification and control within its QMS, but should not be presumed to reproduce every NQA-1 independence provision.

Strategic point: Evaluate actual roles, authority and verification responsibility.

18Can ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation support nuclear acceptance?

It can be relevant evidence where a permitted regulatory and procurement route recognizes the specific accreditation scope. NQA-1-2026 introduces accreditation-related provisions, but publication does not establish automatic NRC acceptance. Accreditation does not replace project-specific obligations.

Strategic point: Confirm the accepted route before relying on accreditation.

19What is changing in software QA?

Software QA is longstanding in nuclear assurance. The 2026 NQA-1 edition restructures Subpart 2.7 and related guidance and adds guidance about changes in the operating environment. The central objective remains controlled requirements, qualification, configuration, verification, validation and changes.

Strategic point: Treat software changes as technical configuration changes.

20What makes objective evidence credible?

Records must connect the applicable requirement with the performed work, result and acceptance decision. Their technical adequacy, identification, traceability, authenticity and retrievability matter more than volume. All mandatory records still must be created and retained.

Strategic point: More paperwork is not automatically more assurance.

Effectiveness and integration

21How should counterfeit, fraudulent and suspect items be addressed?

Use supplier due diligence, authenticity verification, traceability, inspection, escalation and controlled disposition. ISO 19443 explicitly addresses counterfeit, fraudulent and suspect items. NQA-1 procurement and nonconformance controls also provide relevant mechanisms.

Strategic point: Prevent unsuitable items from entering the acceptance chain.

22Does closing a nonconformance demonstrate effective corrective action?

No. Disposition of an affected item and elimination of a recurring cause are distinct tasks. Corrective action effectiveness must be evaluated using relevant evidence, including recurrence, implementation and verified resolution.

Strategic point: Track resolved causes, not only closed reports.

23Do completed audits prove that the audited activities are effective?

No. Audits have long been intended to assess implementation and programme effectiveness. NQA-1-2026 refines audit-related provisions, but effectiveness itself is not a newly introduced expectation. Credible conclusions require adequate scope, execution and objective evidence.

Strategic point: Audit completion is an activity; assurance requires credible results.

24How should quality risk influence programme delivery?

A useful decision-oriented approach is to track unresolved technical requirements before procurement release, qualifications and inspection planning before execution, and evidence completeness before acceptance. These checks support risk reduction but are not substitute requirements for approved project QA programmes.

Strategic point: Make QA risks visible at delivery decision gates.

25What is the strategic optimization of NQA-1, ISO 9001 and ISO 19443?

The practical optimization is to share compatible management processes while preserving nuclear-specific controls, then make requirements, supplier capability and evidence visible at the decisions they support. A three-gate management model can organize these checks without adding unnecessary independent assessments.

Strategic point: Optimize processes without weakening requirements or interface control.

II-A. Classification terminology: the hidden delivery risk for European programmes

A familiar term is not necessarily an equivalent classification. European nuclear programmes increasingly connect U.S.-designed technology, IAEA-informed safety principles, European suppliers and national licensing requirements. Similar terms can legitimately have different boundaries.

Regulatory precision: 10 CFR 50.2 defines U.S. Safety-Related SSCs, but does not supply a universal definition of Important to Safety for every NRC-regulated application. NRC GL 84-01 discusses broader power-reactor ITS usage; 10 CFR 72.3 separately defines ITS for spent-fuel storage and casks.

Important: “ITNS” is not a generic classification defined in U.S. NRC 10 CFR 50.2. In U.S. nuclear regulation, “important to safety” also has specific meanings outside Part 50, including spent-fuel storage under 10 CFR 72.3.

Why do the definitions differ?

  1. Regulatory function: U.S. SR refers to SSCs credited for specified design-basis safety functions; broader ITS usage addresses additional safety-significant features.
  2. IAEA safety taxonomy: IAEA distinguishes safety systems, features for design extension conditions and other safety related items. The original Figure 3, p. 152, and definitions on p. 153 illustrate this difference. The figure is linked, not reproduced.
  3. Supplier QMS purpose: ISO 19443 uses ITNS to determine nuclear-specific QMS obligations for products, services, items and activities.
  4. National licensing basis: IAEA publications help establish common safety concepts but do not independently replace national law, licensing conditions or approved project classifications.

Illustrative terminology case, not an equipment classification decision: A reactor protection actuation function may be performed by a U.S. Safety-Related SSC. In the IAEA equipment taxonomy, the corresponding function belongs to a safety system, not the separate IAEA safety related items group. ISO 19443 may treat relevant products or services as ITNS. The three labels describe different aspects of safety and assurance; the project-approved design classification remains decisive.

Why it matters to European nuclear programme delivery

Risk arises when unapproved word-for-word equivalence determines technical or contractual requirements. The result may be both under-control (missed QA or verification obligations) and over-control (unnecessary qualification and assessment effort).

Five-step classification-to-assurance crosswalk

  1. Identify the safety function: credited function, design-basis conditions, consequences of failure.
  2. Record original classification and authority: preserve the U.S., IAEA-informed or national/project term; do not rewrite a design decision through translation.
  3. Derive applicable requirements: technical codes, nuclear QA provisions, inspection independence, special processes, records and licensing commitments.
  4. Flow down to the supplier: technical specifications, purchase orders, scope qualification, inspection/test plans and acceptance criteria.
  5. Verify at delivery gates: procurement release, execution release and acceptance each require appropriate evidence.

This crosswalk is an analytical and procurement governance tool, not permission to reclassify equipment. III. European supplier development: three pathways

A supplier's quality development strategy should start from its intended products, services, activities and customer requirements, not from an assumed certificate hierarchy. A component may be described differently under U.S., ISO and IAEA terminology; the governing requirements follow the approved project basis.

Pathway 1: ISO 9001 for industrial and NSR capability

ISO 9001 certification is a practical foundation for non-safety-related (NSR) manufacturing, construction and services, where appropriate. It supports process control, competence, procurement, records, nonconformance management and improvement. It is not a universally mandatory condition for every NSR contract, and NSR classification does not remove requirements governing activities that affect safety-related work.

Pathway 2: ISO 19443 for ITNS nuclear supply-chain capability

ISO 19443 is well suited to organizations supplying products and services important to nuclear safety under an ISO-based customer framework. It adds nuclear safety culture, ITNS determination, a graded approach and nuclear-specific supply-chain controls. Prior separate ISO 9001 certification is commercially useful but not always a prerequisite to ISO 19443 certification. Suppliers transitioning their corporate QMS to ISO 9001:2026 must preserve the relevant ISO 9001:2015 requirements embedded in ISO 19443:2018.

Pathway 3: Applicable NQA-1 controls for NQA-1-based SR scope

Where safety-related (SR) engineering, manufacturing, construction, inspection, testing, software or services are procured under an NQA-1-based framework, suppliers must implement the applicable edition and requirements, together with technical codes, customer and regulatory obligations. NQA-1 is not universally required for every European SR scope.

Certification distinction: ASME NQA-1 Quality Program Certification differs from ASME Section III Nuclear Component Certification. One does not substitute for the other. See ASME NQA-1 programme and ASME nuclear component certification.

Three development pathways, not three universally mandatory certification steps.

IV. Poland: a current supplier qualification example

On 13 October 2026, Westinghouse announced conditional purchase orders for safety-related AP1000 structural modules with Energomontaż-Północ Gdynia, FAMAK, Mostostal Kielce and Mostostal Kraków. The announcement links the full awards to NQA-1 certification and successful fabrication of pre-production qualification mockups. Read the Westinghouse release.

This is a concrete illustration of two different but complementary questions: does the supplier operate the required nuclear QA programme, and can it demonstrate the technical manufacturing capability necessary for the assigned scope?

The example does not mean every European safety-related contract requires NQA-1. It means the qualification pathway must follow the governing procurement requirements and the actual intended product or service.

V. Optimize processes without weakening assurance

Optimization is not a request to weaken NQA-1, ISO 19443, technical codes or approved QA programmes. It is a proposal to align assurance activities with decisions that create technical and commercial exposure. A second review adds value only when it addresses a distinct requirement, risk or verification purpose.

Three evidence-based readiness gates

The following Three-Gate Assurance Model is a proposed governance tool, not a mandated ASME, ISO or regulatory checkpoint and not a replacement for contractual hold points. It should be adapted to project sequencing, engineering maturity and the actual classification of work.

Worked example: where terminology changes a procurement decision

Hypothetical training case, not a finding about any real vendor, reactor design or Polish project. A European ISO 9001-certified supplier bids to assemble an electrical cabinet for a credited safety actuation function in a U.S.-designed reactor. One customer document calls the relevant SSC Safety-Related (U.S. terminology); an IAEA-informed design note groups the function under a Safety System; the supplier evaluates the product and work as ITNS for ISO 19443 purposes. These statements answer different questions. None modifies the approved classification.

Blindly translating U.S. Safety-Related to IAEA Safety Related Item would be incorrect where the equipment belongs to an IAEA Safety System. Conversely, ISO ITNS designation cannot displace a specified NQA-1-based QA programme.

What changes in practice? A supplier is not forced to obtain an ISO 19443 certificate merely because the IAEA terminology appears, nor can contractual NQA-1 requirements be omitted because the supplier calls the product ITNS. The actual approved framework determines the controls. A capability gap can be addressed before execution rather than at final acceptance.

The intended effects are earlier clarification, less preventable rework and more predictable acceptance. These are management hypotheses, not guaranteed savings. The gates do not replace approved design reviews, regulatory approvals or contractual inspection hold points.

VI. Assurance Continuity: the strategic objective

Assurance Continuity is a proposed management concept: preserving applicable requirements, responsibilities, verification obligations and objective evidence as work moves between organizations, contracts, disciplines and project stages. It is not a separately defined obligation within ASME NQA-1 or ISO 19443.

Assurance continuity is not simply a traceability database. It is a governance discipline: each responsible organization has to know which requirements it is implementing, what constitutes conformity, what evidence is required, and which decisions that evidence enables. The model respects the distinct obligations of licensees, designers, purchasers and suppliers rather than creating a fictional single programme-wide QMS.

A practical continuity chain looks like this:

Safety function›Classification›Technical requirements›Contractual flow-down›Execution›Verification›Acceptance evidence

Different independent organizations may hold different certificates and operate different QMSs. A programme therefore needs a federated assurance architecture: distinct organizational responsibilities joined by controlled requirements, configuration, verification and acceptance interfaces.

The quality of an interface cannot be inferred solely from the quality certificates on either side of it.

VII. Strategic conclusion

The central choice is not between rigorous QA and efficient delivery. Both are necessary. European programmes must also ensure that differences among U.S. SR/ITS, ISO ITNS and IAEA terminology do not become gaps in procurement requirements, verification or acceptance evidence. ASME NQA-1, ISO 9001 and ISO 19443 serve different scopes and contractual frameworks; none is a universal replacement for the others.

For European suppliers, quality capability should develop in relation to the work they intend to perform: an industrial QMS foundation for applicable non-safety-related supply, ISO 19443 capability for relevant ITNS work, and the appropriate NQA-1 programme for contracts that invoke it. These are complementary pathways, not compulsory sequential certificates.

Do not ask only whether the quality system is certified. Ask whether the next project decision is supported by competent, timely and traceable evidence.

THE GOAL IS NOT FEWER CONTROLS. IT IS FEWER UNCONTROLLED RISKS, LESS AVOIDABLE REWORK AND MORE RELIABLE NUCLEAR PROGRAMME DELIVERY.

The Three-Gate Assurance Model is a practical way to test this proposition at procurement release, execution release and acceptance. Used alongside the governing QA programme, it helps turn standards compliance into evidence-based delivery decisions without reducing nuclear safety obligations.
