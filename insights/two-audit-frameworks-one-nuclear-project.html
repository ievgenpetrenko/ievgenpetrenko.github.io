Two Audit Frameworks. One Nuclear Project. Who Owns the Gaps?

ASME NQA-1 and ISO 19443: from audit compliance to industrial readiness and nuclear programme delivery in Europe

By Ievgen Petrenko | Published 12 October 2026 | Independent Professional Insight

Executive perspective

Europe's nuclear ambitions extend beyond delivering individual power plants. They include building an industrial base capable of supporting large nuclear power plant (NPP) programmes and repeatable deployment of small modular reactors (SMRs).

The challenge is not only technology, investment, engineering capacity or manufacturing. It is also the ability to establish coherent nuclear assurance across different regulatory traditions, suppliers, quality standards and contractual interfaces.

ASME NQA-1 and ISO 19443 may be applied within the same European nuclear supply chain. Their requirements overlap, but their audit criteria, scopes and conclusions cannot simply be exchanged. A supplier can hold a relevant ISO 19443 certificate while a customer still needs objective evidence against specific NQA-1 obligations. Conversely, NQA-1 compliance within a defined scope does not establish conformity with every ISO 19443 requirement.

Several audits can be satisfactory individually while the project lacks evidence needed for a particular technical acceptance decision. That gap can trigger late verification, rework, delays or disputes over responsibility. For SMRs, a weakness may be repeated across manufacturing batches and multiple units.

The strategic challenge is not which standard is superior. It is whether a nuclear programme can connect applicable requirements, safety classification, audits, technical verification and acceptance into one accountable delivery system.

This analysis moves from that governance question into practical supplier-audit checklists, classification interfaces and implications for large NPP and SMR industrial programmes.

1. Why this matters for Europe's nuclear industrial strategy

The European Commission's SMR Strategy of 10 March 2026 emphasizes industrial supply chains, cooperation, standardization and fleet deployment. Large NPP construction likewise depends on the mobilization of engineering, manufacturing and construction capabilities across several countries.

Many potential European nuclear suppliers already operate sophisticated processes in power generation, aerospace, machinery, automation, materials or other regulated industries. Yet technical capability and an established quality management system are not necessarily sufficient for a particular nuclear procurement contract.

A project may impose additional controls on design verification, supplier flow-down, inspection, testing, nonconforming items, traceability and retained records. These requirements must be defined before manufacturing, not rediscovered when an item is ready to ship.

Two extremes are undesirable: over-specification, which creates needless expense and barriers to supplier entry, and under-specification, which leaves safety or acceptance requirements inadequately controlled. A credible assurance architecture helps distinguish justified controls from duplication while safeguarding nuclear safety.

2. The regulatory and contractual baseline comes first

Neither NQA-1 nor ISO 19443, by itself, establishes the licensing basis of an EU nuclear installation. The national regulatory framework, the design and safety case, licensing commitments, relevant technical codes and procurement contracts determine what applies.

Directive 2009/71/Euratom, as amended by Directive 2014/87/Euratom, preserves the licence holder's primary responsibility for nuclear safety, including relevant contractor and subcontractor activities. Its management-system expectations cannot be delegated away through supplier certification.

NQA-1 can be invoked through a technology supplier's requirements, a project's approved QA programme, contract clauses or relevant licensing arrangements. ISO 19443 establishes quality management system requirements for organizations supplying products and services important to nuclear safety (ITNS). ISO specifically states that its requirements are complementary to, not alternatives to, customer and applicable regulatory requirements. Its application to activities on a licensed nuclear site also requires prior agreement by the licence holder [ISO 19443].

Use of US-origin reactor technology does not automatically make every US regulation directly applicable under European law. The project must determine which requirements apply, their invoked editions, their flow-down and the organizations authorized to verify or accept conformity.

Edition control matters: ASME has published NQA-1:2026, and ISO 19443:2018 has Amendment 1:2024. Existing contracts or accepted QA programmes may invoke earlier editions. Publication of a new edition does not automatically change those obligations.

3. Two frameworks, different audit conclusions

NQA-1 Part I contains 18 fundamental QA requirements governing organization, design, procurement, work instructions, documents, purchased items, identification, processes, inspection, testing, equipment, handling, status, nonconformities, corrective action, records and audits. Requirement 18 addresses auditing.

ISO 19443 builds on ISO 9001 with nuclear-sector requirements addressing ITNS scope, graded controls, nuclear safety culture, leadership, organizational competence, supplier management and controlled operations. Clause 9.2 covers internal audits. Third-party ISO 19443 certification involves the certification-body framework of ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/TS 23406:2024.

Audit dimension ASME NQA-1 ISO 19443
Primary purpose Verify applicable nuclear QA programme requirements and implementation Assess QMS conformity and effectiveness for ITNS activities
Typical criteria Invoked NQA-1 edition, QA programme, procedures and contract ISO 19443, organizational QMS, relevant customer and regulatory obligations
Audit emphasis Specified QA controls, verification and objective evidence Organizational processes, ITNS controls, competence and QMS effectiveness
Supplier oversight Procurement control, supplier assessment and verification of purchased items and services External-provider capability, selection, monitoring and effectiveness
Independence NQA-1 auditor qualification and independence requirements Impartiality and competence, with additional certification-body requirements where applicable
What a positive result establishes Conformity within the defined NQA-1 audit scope and criteria Conformity within the defined ISO audit scope and criteria
What it does not establish alone Every ISO 19443 requirement or final product acceptance Every NQA-1 obligation or acceptance of a particular nuclear component

The standards should not be described as simply technical versus managerial, or as universally stricter versus weaker. Both can require detailed investigation of actual engineering and manufacturing records. The distinction is the requirement against which each piece of evidence is evaluated.

An internal audit, a second-party customer audit, a third-party certification audit, technical surveillance and an item inspection are different forms of assurance. Coordinating them can be useful, but their purposes and decision authorities remain distinct.

4. Safety-Related and ITNS: the classification boundary

Before finalizing any supplier audit checklist, a project must know what safety function is involved, how the item or activity is classified and which requirements follow from that classification.

4.1 Safety-Related in the US regulatory context

Under 10 CFR 50.2, the US definition of safety-related structures, systems and components (SSCs) concerns SSCs relied upon to remain functional during and after design-basis events to assure the integrity of the reactor coolant pressure boundary, safe reactor shutdown, or the prevention or mitigation of certain accident consequences.

This is a design-basis and safety-function concept. NQA-1 does not itself assign safety classes. It sets requirements for QA programmes and controlled activities when invoked within the applicable scope. In the US, 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B establishes fundamental nuclear QA criteria; its direct applicability must not be presumed for an EU project.

4.2 ITNS in ISO 19443

ISO 19443 defines Important to Nuclear Safety (ITNS) by reference to the characteristics of a product, service, item or activity whose failure could cause undue radiation exposure of people or the environment (Clause 3.5).

This extends beyond a particular category of US-classified safety-related SSCs and can involve engineering, manufacturing, testing or other services. ISO 19443 uses graded controls appropriate to the identified significance. ITNS status is not an automatic US safety-related classification and does not independently invoke NQA-1 requirements.

4.3 Why European classification adds another interface

The IAEA SSG-30 provides guidance on classifying SSCs important to safety through functions and significance. National systems, reactor designs and contracts can use other classification arrangements.

A European project should not assume Safety-Related = ITNS = National Safety Class. An explicit, approved mapping is needed, and classification must remain connected to the plant's safety case and applicable rules.

Question US Safety-Related concept ISO 19443 ITNS concept
Main focus Defined SSC safety functions in the US design-basis regulatory context Nuclear-safety significance of products, services, items and activities
What determines its scope? Functions credited under relevant design and regulatory definitions Potential nuclear-safety consequences within ISO 19443's scope
Does it classify the same things? Primarily SSCs Can include products, services and activities
Does the term automatically trigger NQA-1? No. QA obligations follow the applicable framework and invoked requirements No. ITNS designation is not contractual invocation of NQA-1

4.4 Illustrative classification examples

Item or activity Potential safety-related position Potential ITNS position Key audit question
Valve credited for safe shutdown Could be safety-related, according to its actual safety function Expected to be ITNS if ISO criteria apply Has its safety function been translated into correct procurement and QA controls?
Radiation-monitoring equipment not credited for a US design-basis safety function May not meet the US safety-related definition May still be ITNS depending on consequences of failure Were safety-significant customer requirements retained?

Each example depends on the actual reactor design, service function, safety case, national rules and contract. The table is not a classification decision.

The strategic risk runs in both directions. Under-classification can omit necessary QA or verification. Over-classification can create unjustified cost, constrain suppliers and delay procurement. For SMRs, a mistaken classification or procurement rule may be replicated across a fleet.

Classification is only useful when it leads to the correct technical controls, verification and acceptance authority.

5. Practical checklist A: an NQA-1 audit of an ISO 19443-certified manufacturer

Illustrative scenario: A European manufacturer holds ISO 19443 certification and has received a purchase order invoking selected NQA-1 requirements for a safety-significant valve actuator assembly. The customer is conducting a second-party supplier audit. Its QA programme and contractual scope govern the audit.

The following is an original illustrative checklist, organized by the 18 requirements of NQA-1 Part I. It is not a reproduction or complete substitute for any published standard. Actual questions and applicability depend on the invoked edition, audit objectives, relevant Part II provisions and product or service scope.

NQA-1 Part I Typical supplier-audit question Representative evidence
1. Organization Who can stop nonconforming work, and how is verification independence established? Organization chart; delegated authorities; QA responsibilities
2. QA Programme Where are the invoked NQA-1 requirements implemented in the existing management system? Approved QA programme; requirements matrix; training and qualifications
3. Design Control How are design inputs, verification independence and changes controlled? Calculations; verification records; change approvals
4. Procurement Document Control Were nuclear QA requirements transmitted to lower-tier suppliers? Purchase orders; specifications; QA clauses; reviews
5. Instructions, Procedures and Drawings Does controlled work documentation define adequate criteria and methods? Work instructions; procedures; current drawings
6. Document Control How is use of superseded documentation prevented? Registers; distribution records; revision control
7. Control of Purchased Items and Services How is supplier capability established and purchased-item conformity verified? Supplier assessments; source/receipt inspection; acceptance evidence
8. Identification and Control of Items Can a sampled item be traced to required material and manufacturing records? Serial/heat/lot numbers; certificates; traceability records
9. Control of Special Processes Are applicable special processes performed by qualified personnel using qualified procedures? Welding/NDE qualifications; procedure approvals; execution records
10. Inspection Are inspections performed by qualified persons against approved criteria? Inspection and test plans; reports; inspection status
11. Test Control Are tests performed under approved conditions and acceptance criteria? Test procedures; reports; deviations
12. Measuring and Test Equipment If an instrument is found out of tolerance, how are previous results evaluated? Calibration records; equipment history; impact assessment
13. Handling, Storage and Shipping Are required handling, preservation and storage conditions maintained? Handling procedures; preservation and shipping records
14. Inspection, Test and Operating Status How are items prevented from proceeding before required hold points are released? Status labels; hold-point records; authorizations
15. Nonconforming Items Who may authorize a repair or use-as-is disposition? NCRs; technical evaluations; authorized dispositions
16. Corrective Action How are significant conditions adverse to quality analyzed and recurrence prevented? Cause analysis; corrective actions; effectiveness reviews
17. QA Records Can the complete required record set for a selected item be retrieved? Manufacturing dossier; records index; retention controls
18. Audits Are applicable NQA-1 requirements audited by qualified and independent personnel? Audit plans; qualifications; reports; follow-up

Seven especially revealing questions

  1. Show the NQA-1-to-procedure cross-reference. Which invoked requirements are already satisfied by the ISO 19443 QMS, and which require additional controls?
  2. Trace one real order from contract review to final records. Do the same specifications and revisions appear across engineering, procurement, manufacturing, inspection and release?
  3. Show the independent verification of a sampled design change. Did the reviewer and method satisfy the particular project's requirements?
  4. Trace a nuclear QA requirement into a lower-tier purchase order. Can the manufacturer demonstrate both flow-down and verification?
  5. Explain an actual out-of-tolerance calibration case. Were potentially affected inspections identified and evaluated?
  6. Show who authorized a nonconforming-item disposition. Were the necessary engineering and customer approvals obtained?
  7. Demonstrate how the audit programme covers the invoked NQA-1 criteria. ISO 19443 certification reports alone do not establish that coverage.

An auditor should sample real activities, not stop with procedure existence. A document can be formally approved while the actual item, manufacturing revision or inspection acceptance criterion is incorrect.

An ISO 19443 auditor can also identify failures to comply with customer-imposed NQA-1 requirements when those obligations are within the assessed QMS and sample. It would be incorrect to characterize ISO 19443 as a documentation-only audit or to presume its auditors ignore technical records.

The important difference is not always the evidence examined. It is the specific acceptance criterion and compliance conclusion applied to that evidence.

7. Where the same evidence can support different conclusions

Sampled evidence ISO 19443 perspective Additional NQA-1 question
Design change package Is the design-control process effectively implemented? Were specified verification independence, approval and change requirements met?
Supplier evaluation Is supplier selection and monitoring effective? Does the evaluation address all applicable nuclear procurement controls?
Purchase order Were customer requirements reviewed and communicated? Were prescribed QA clauses correctly transferred to lower tiers?
Inspection report Was inspection controlled and recorded? Were required inspectors, hold points and acceptance criteria used?
NCR and disposition Was nonconforming output controlled? Was disposition authority correct, and were affected items and QA records addressed?
Document system Is documented information effectively controlled? Can the specified item-specific QA records be recovered and authenticated?

The outcome may differ even when the same document is examined. A purchase order may demonstrate an effective general supplier-control process but omit an additional nuclear verification clause. Conversely, a complete NQA-1 procurement package alone cannot prove the effectiveness of all organizational ISO 19443 processes.

Evidence can be shared. Audit criteria, safety classifications and acceptance authority cannot be assumed to be interchangeable.

8. Hypothetical audit result: a certified supplier with unresolved project gaps

The ISO 19443-certified valve-actuator manufacturer is audited against the customer's invoked NQA-1 requirements. Its management system is functioning, but the customer samples an order with a recent design modification.

Observation Appropriate preliminary interpretation
ISO 19443 certificate is valid and relevant to scope Supporting evidence of QMS capability, not an item acceptance decision
Training and material-traceability samples are satisfactory No deficiency detected in the samples examined
Required independent design verification cannot be demonstrated Potential NQA-1 Requirement 3 / project nonconformity
A lower-tier order omits specified QA verification obligations Potential Requirements 4 and 7 / procurement gap
Internal audits did not assess the additional contractual NQA-1 obligations Possible audit programme or QA scope deficiency, depending on requirements

The correct conclusion is not automatically that the ISO certificate is invalid, that the item is defective, or that every NQA-1 requirement failed. Findings must be supported by the applicable criteria and actual objective evidence.

An ISO 19443 auditor could identify the same deficiency if the relevant contractual obligation and activity were sampled. Different audit outcomes can result from different scopes and samples without either audit being inherently deficient.

A verification gap discovered before manufacturing may be relatively straightforward to resolve. Discovered after fabrication, it can require engineering evaluation of affected items, new evidence, corrective action and possibly supplementary testing or inspection. Shipment, installation or acceptance can become constrained even when the hardware is complete.

This is the delivery consequence of assurance discovered too late.

9. Commercial-grade dedication and other special obligations

Where applicable, commercial-grade dedication (CGD) is an item or service acceptance process, not a substitute term for supplier certification. It relies on critical characteristics and the means to verify them to establish reasonable assurance of the intended safety function, under the governing requirements.

ISO 19443 includes concepts concerning commercial-grade items and activities; its definition notes that identifying critical characteristics and their acceptance is the customer's responsibility. NQA-1 programmes may invoke specific dedication provisions, including relevant Part II requirements. Applicable US regulatory guidance can be relevant to the technical approach when required by the project.

An ISO 19443 certificate does not dedicate a particular item by itself. Equally, a dedicated item does not demonstrate that every aspect of a manufacturer's wider management system meets ISO 19443. See also the existing portfolio analysis: NQA-1 vs ISO 19443 and Commercial Grade Dedication.

Other programme-specific obligations may involve software QA, specialized testing, construction activities, national conformity assessment, technical codes or authorized inspection. A complete audit plan must identify the scope actually applicable to the supplier.

10. Large NPPs: managing the interfaces

A large NPP may have many tiers of design, procurement, manufacturing, site construction, inspection and commissioning organizations. A requirement can start in the safety case, move into design documents, enter procurement specifications, be allocated to a manufacturer and eventually be demonstrated through inspection records and final acceptance.

Each handover introduces risk: incomplete flow-down, outdated revisions, inconsistent classifications, unclear verification independence or missing approval authority. Individual suppliers may have satisfactory management systems while the programme loses control at an interface.

A useful assurance architecture connects design and configuration control with supplier qualification, procurement documentation, manufacturing oversight, construction records and commissioning evidence. Audit findings should be evaluated for consequences beyond the audited organization, including downstream work and schedule milestones.

The objective is not a single giant audit or a larger quantity of audit reports. It is an accountable route from safety requirement to decision-grade evidence.

11. SMRs: repeating production must not repeat assurance failures

SMR delivery models may move substantial construction work into manufacturing facilities and use standardized modules, repeat orders and fleet-style deployments. Standardization can support learning, efficiency and supply chain investment.

It can also propagate errors. A flawed supplier process, incorrect specification or poorly controlled design change may affect several manufactured modules before detection. A certification audit or supplier qualification undertaken for one contract may not automatically remain sufficient for another design revision, site or licensing framework.

Programme dimension Large NPP SMR or fleet model
Main interfaces Multitier EPC, manufacturing and site construction Factory, modular integration, several units and sites
Critical QA challenge Requirements flow-down and construction acceptance Reuse of evidence with configuration and production control
Typical change risk Design or site-interface change affecting a project Common change affecting a manufacturing series
Supplier qualification Primarily contract- and project-specific Potential for controlled reuse, subject to defined conditions
Corrective-action reach Related packages and project activities Multiple modules, batches and deployments
Decision responsibility Defined licence-holder and project authorities Defined authorities for each module, site and licence holder

The WENRA safety objectives for new NPPs remain a minimum for SMRs. Repeated manufacturing does not reduce the need for credible safety assurance.

The strategic opportunity is conditional reuse of valid assurance evidence: retain common records where scope, configuration, supplier capability and obligations still match; perform additional verification where they do not. This is not automatic cross-border regulatory recognition.

12. Three levels of supplier readiness

Organizational readiness. Does the supplier possess the competence, facilities, management processes and financial and technical capacity for nuclear work? ISO 19443 certification may support this assessment.

Contractual readiness. Does the supplier understand the particular order's safety classification, engineering codes, applicable NQA-1 controls, supplier flow-down and verification obligations? Contract-specific gap analysis may be required.

Delivery readiness. Has the supplier actually completed the required activities and produced suitable traceable evidence for the product or service, including inspections, tests, nonconformity disposition and release?

A company can be organizationally capable without being ready for a particular nuclear contract. It can also be qualified for the contract but not yet ready to deliver a specific item. Treating these as distinct decisions gives industry clearer entry routes and projects better control of acceptance risk.

13. Six stages of integrated assurance governance

Stage 1. Establish the requirements baseline

Identify the applicable national rules, safety functions and classifications, invoked editions, project QA programme, technical codes, procurement obligations and relevant licensing commitments. Assign responsibility for interpretation and change control.

Stage 2. Map requirements without assuming equivalence

Create a controlled matrix of project obligations, NQA-1 requirements, ISO 19443 processes, implementing procedures and evidence. Show where coverage is complete, partly demonstrated or requires additional controls.

Stage 3. Coordinate the audits

Where justified, align supplier visits, interview planning and evidence samples. Retain separate audit criteria, appropriately competent and independent personnel, findings and conclusions where required. Do not treat a certification audit as an automatic replacement for a purchaser's technical verification.

Stage 4. Connect evidence to configuration

Trace significant records to the correct specification, revision, manufactured item and acceptance requirement. For SMRs, extend to batches, modules and affected configurations across sites where applicable.

Stage 5. Define acceptance decision points

Clarify what must be demonstrated before fabrication release, shipment, installation, commissioning and final acceptance. The authority to close an audit finding is not automatically the authority to accept an item or change its design basis.

Stage 6. Address systemic findings

Evaluate significant nonconformities for cross-supplier, cross-contract and fleet implications. Corrective action should cover affected items and relevant causes, not merely close the immediate audit record.

14. Measuring what matters

Audit counts and corrective-action closure rates are useful management indicators, but they do not alone demonstrate assurance effectiveness. Programme leaders should also monitor whether significant requirements and risks are controlled at decision points.

Suggested indicator Leadership question
Critical requirements allocated before contract award Do suppliers know the obligations before pricing and planning?
Suppliers assessed against specific nuclear contract scope Does the qualified-supplier list reflect real procurement readiness?
Significant assurance gaps remaining at manufacturing release Are problems being detected early enough?
Technical acceptance deficiencies discovered after manufacture Are assurance failures creating avoidable late work?
Time and quality of disposition for significant findings Are decisions timely and technically defensible?
Duplicated assessments of essentially the same evidence Can audit resources be coordinated without lost coverage?
Systemic findings across batches or SMR units Are corrective actions reaching the full affected population?
Project milestones affected by assurance deficiencies What is the impact on delivery predictability?

Measures should not reward fewer findings at any cost or rushed corrective-action closure. An effective programme may initially identify more problems because it is better at detecting them. The objective is early detection, complete technical evaluation and prevention of recurrence.

15. Strategic recommendations for European nuclear programmes

  1. Start with safety functions and approved classification. Without them, a standards cross-reference may harmonize documents while missing actual technical obligations.
  2. Define the NQA-1 and ISO 19443 relationship before procurement. Clarify invoked editions, programme scope, grading, supplier obligations and acceptance authority.
  3. Treat certification as valuable input, not universal authorization. Verify certificate scope and the additional requirements of the specific contract.
  4. Avoid unnecessary duplicate audits. Reuse valid evidence and coordinate compatible assessment activities without weakening independence or required verification.
  5. Connect QA findings to engineering and project controls. Evaluate configuration, procurement, manufacturing, construction and commissioning consequences.
  6. Build supplier development pathways. Help capable European manufacturers understand organizational, contractual and delivery readiness before major commitments.
  7. Develop fleet-aware assurance for SMRs. Track common processes, configuration baselines, manufacturing batches and potentially affected modules across deployments.

Conclusion: an audit is valuable only when it supports the right decision

Europe does not have to choose between ASME NQA-1 and ISO 19443. The relevant task is to establish how both can operate coherently under applicable national regulation and project obligations.

For large NPPs, the main difficulty is often controlling complex interfaces among the licence holder, technology vendor, EPC organization, suppliers and site delivery teams. For SMRs, that difficulty extends to repeated manufacturing, fleet configuration and the valid reuse of evidence.

The objective is not more audits, fewer audits, or one certificate that supposedly covers everything. It is an assurance system in which every significant requirement has an owner, an appropriate verification method and a defensible route to acceptance.

Supplier certification, contract qualification, compliance auditing, technical verification and product acceptance should reinforce each other without being confused.

The real measure of audit integration is not the number of assessments completed. It is whether a nuclear programme can demonstrate that nothing essential has been lost between them.

Methodological note

This is an independent professional analysis based on publicly available information. Audit questions, comparisons, supplier observations and indicators are original illustrative examples, not official ASME or ISO checklists, regulatory decisions or a complete audit plan. Exact audit criteria must be established from the authorized texts of the invoked standard editions, applicable NQA-1 supplementary provisions, procurement requirements and national licensing arrangements. Safety-Related, ITNS and national safety classifications require project-specific interpretation and must not be equated automatically.
